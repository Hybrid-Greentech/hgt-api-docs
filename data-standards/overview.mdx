## Scope

These pages define what you publish, how often you publish it, and how quickly an asset or resource must respond to a dispatch. If another page or diagram states a different value, use the value here.

The requirements cover every integration surface:

| Integration | What these pages govern |
|-------------|-------------------------|
| Delegated Operators API | All metrics, bounds, and compliance rules |
| Distributed Energy Resources API | All metrics, bounds, and compliance rules |
| EV charger and heat pump v1 APIs | Bounds and compliance rules, applied to the metrics those deprecated APIs already carry |
| Direct PPC and OEM hardware | Bounds, applied over the registers in the [Signal List](/controller/signal-list) |

You are responsible for meeting these requirements. Hybrid Greentech verifies them in the sandbox [integration test](/data-standards/compliance#integration-test) and when investigating an operational incident. The platform does not publish a compliance feed, so the absence of a warning is not confirmation that an integration is compliant.

## Two independent keys

Two keys determine every requirement, and they compose. Neither one alone tells you what to do.

| Key | Determines | Defined in |
|-----|------------|------------|
| Device type, or resource type | **Which** metrics you publish | [Metrics](/data-standards/metrics) |
| Market | **How fast** those metrics flow, and how fast the asset or resource responds | [Market requirements](/data-standards/markets) |

A battery and a heat pump publish different metrics. The same battery publishes the same metrics every 100 ms when delivering FFR and every minute when delivering mFRR.

### Requirement resolution

Bounds come from the **strictest market an asset or resource is prequalified for**. They do not change when a task starts or ends. Data frequency lives in edge configuration, so an asset that sells FCR-D in the morning and mFRR in the afternoon holds the FCR-D data frequency all day.

Two consequences:

- You configure the integration once, against a known set of markets.
- Compliance windows never straddle a change in data frequency, so every window is assessable.

An asset or resource that is not prequalified for any ancillary market can serve `wholesale` only, and takes the `wholesale` bounds.

<Note>
  These requirements replace the earlier device-type-keyed rates. Any page that ties a data frequency or a response bound to asset class rather than to market is superseded.
</Note>

## Control topology

The platform measures **roundtrip asset response** from a setpoint it sent. That gives a single rule:

<Info>
  A roundtrip bound applies to an asset or a resource if and only if the platform issues activations to it.
</Info>

| Arrangement | What the platform sends | Roundtrip applies |
|-------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Platform control | Tasks **and** activations carrying setpoints | Yes |
| Local control | Tasks only — you compute the response yourself | No |

Under local control you answer to the TSO's activation profile directly. The platform verifies delivery against that profile rather than against a roundtrip bound. Data frequency applies either way: the platform needs the telemetry to verify delivery and, for aFRR, to report it onward.

For assets integrated through the Delegated Operators API:

- **aFRR is always platform control.** The PICASSO signal arrives at Hybrid Greentech on a four-second cycle, so the platform relays it as activations.
- **Every other market may be local.** FCR-family products are frequency-triggered and can be computed on site, provided the asset meets the TSO profile.

Frequency-responsive markets under platform control require Hybrid Greentech's own frequency measurement and setpoint computation on site. A sub-second response cannot be closed over a cloud roundtrip.

Resources are always under platform control. The platform issues them setpoints directly, with no task layer, so the roundtrip bound always applies. A resource additionally acknowledges every activation it does not discard as stale, which separates a refusal to actuate from a lost link — see [Compliance](/data-standards/compliance#transport).

## New obligations

Some requirements on these pages are new or tighter than what your integration was built against. The most likely to affect you:

- Available power up and down are now required for every device type on the Delegated Operators API, not for batteries alone.
- Available energy up and down are now required for batteries.
- State of charge tightens from a 5 s to a 1 s bound.
- Grid frequency is mandatory, not optional, for the frequency-responsive markets.
- Resources moving from the v1 EV charger and heat pump APIs take on the v2 metric set. Heat pumps publish `measuredPower` for the first time, EV active power changes sign, and heat pump `availablePowerDown` is recomputed. See [Migrating to v2](/distributed-resources/migration).

Existing integrations remain compliant until their next market prequalification. New integrations meet these requirements from day one.

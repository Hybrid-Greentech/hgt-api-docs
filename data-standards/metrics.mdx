## Reading these tables

Device type decides **which** metrics an asset publishes, and resource type decides which metrics a resource publishes. The market decides **how fast** most of them flow. Where a bound below says "market data frequency", look the value up in [Market requirements](/data-standards/markets).

Sections up to [Baseline power](#baseline-power) name the metrics of the Delegated Operators API, keyed on device type. [Distributed energy resources](#distributed-energy-resources) names the resource registry, keyed on resource type. The two surfaces are separate: they name some quantities differently — active power is `powerAc` on an asset and `measuredPower` on a resource — and neither one's obligations extend to the other.

Publish one measurement per message. Both surfaces carry a single `measurementType` and a single value per message. The platform does not accept batched payloads.

The measurements channel is addressed per asset or per resource:

```
{priceZone}/{customerName}/measurements/{assetId}
{priceZone}/{customerName}/v2/measurements/{resourceId}
```

## Every device type

| Metric | `measurementType` | Type | Unit | Bound |
|--------|-------------------|------|------|-------|
| Active power | `powerAc` | Float | kW | Market data frequency |
| Available power up | `availablePowerUp` | Float | kW | Market data frequency |
| Available power down | `availablePowerDown` | Float | kW | Market data frequency |

Every device type publishes active power and both available power values. Everything else is conditional on the device, the market, or both.

**Up** is upward regulation: the asset injects more power to the grid, by discharging or by reducing consumption. **Down** is downward regulation: the asset absorbs more power from the grid, by charging or by increasing consumption.

Available power tracks measured power, so it carries the market's data frequency.

<Warning>
  Available power up and down are a commitment, not an observation. The platform may dispatch up to the last value you published. If capability falls, publish the new value immediately rather than waiting for the next interval.
</Warning>

## Batteries

| Metric | `measurementType` | Type | Unit | Bound |
|--------|-------------------|------|------|-------|
| Available energy up | `availableEnergyUp` | Float | kWh | 1 s |
| Available energy down | `availableEnergyDown` | Float | kWh | 1 s |
| State of charge | `stateOfCharge` | Float, 0–100 inclusive | % | 1 s |

A battery publishes these in addition to the metrics in [Every device type](#every-device-type).

Available energy and state of charge are market-independent. They describe a slow-moving physical condition, so their bounds hold whether the battery serves FFR or wholesale.

A battery at 94% state of charge has close to full available power up and very little available power down.

Solar plants publish the metrics in [Every device type](#every-device-type) only.

## Grid frequency

| Metric | `measurementType` | Type | Unit | Bound |
|--------|-------------------|------|------|-------|
| Grid frequency | `frequency` | Float | Hz | Market data frequency |

Grid frequency is **mandatory** for the frequency-responsive markets — `ffr`, `fcr-d`, `fcr-n`, and `fcr` — and optional elsewhere. Publish it in other markets when the site measures it.

Publish frequency on each asset's own topic. A site with several assets publishes the same value on every asset's topic, and this duplication is intentional: the platform pairs an asset's power response against the frequency the same controller measured on the same clock.

Grid frequency is a property of the connection point, so the bound does not vary by device type.

## Baseline power

A baseline is the asset's commanded position **excluding** the market being settled. The platform derives delivered energy by subtracting it from measured power.

| Metric | `measurementType` | Type | Unit | Bound |
|--------|-------------------|------|------|-------|
| aFRR baseline | `afrrBaseline` | Float | kW | 1 s |
| mFRR baseline | `mfrrBaseline` | Float | kW | 1 min |

Each bound is its own market's data frequency.

Publish a baseline for each of these markets the asset is enrolled in, and both when it is enrolled in both:

```typescript
afrrBaseline = FCR + FCRD + FCRN + FFR + mFRR + Wholesale
mfrrBaseline = FCR + FCRD + FCRN + FFR + aFRR + Wholesale
```

FCR-family products need no baseline. Their delivered volume is derived from frequency and the committed droop, not by subtraction.

<Warning>
  Never include a market's own setpoint in its baseline. The platform derives delivered energy by subtracting the baseline from measured power, so including it reports zero delivery.
</Warning>

The baseline is piecewise-constant, so holding the last value between samples is expected. Publish immediately whenever any constituent setpoint changes — a step that lands between samples misattributes energy to the settled market.

### Baseline derivation

| Device type | Baseline is | Status |
|-------------|-------------|--------|
| Battery, solar | The sum of commanded setpoints | Exact, and auditable against the activations the platform issued |
| EV charger, heat pump | What the device would have drawn without dispatch | An estimate |

Document your estimation method during onboarding. The platform cannot audit an estimate the way it audits a commanded baseline.

## Distributed energy resources

Resources publish a registry of eight metrics on the `measurements` channel, keyed on **resource type**. Every metric carries one `measurementType` and one `value` per message.

| Metric | `measurementType` | Type | Unit | Periodic bound | Change threshold |
|--------|-------------------|------|------|----------------|------------------|
| Measured power | `measuredPower` | Float, signed | kW | Market data frequency | ±1 kW |
| Available power up | `availablePowerUp` | Float, unsigned | kW | Market data frequency | ±1 kW |
| Available power down | `availablePowerDown` | Float, unsigned | kW | Market data frequency | ±1 kW |
| Site import | `siteImport` | Float, unsigned | kW | 1 s | ±1 kW |
| Site export | `siteExport` | Float, unsigned | kW | 1 s | ±1 kW |
| State of charge | `stateOfCharge` | Float, 0–100 inclusive | % | 1 s | Periodic only |
| Available energy up | `availableEnergyUp` | Float, unsigned | kWh | 1 s | Periodic only |
| Available energy down | `availableEnergyDown` | Float, unsigned | kWh | 1 s | Periodic only |

The obligation is additive:

| `resourceType` | Publishes |
|----------------|-----------|
| `evCharger` | Measured power, available power up, available power down |
| `heatPump` | The above, plus site import and site export |
| `bess` | The above, plus state of charge, available energy up, and available energy down |

The change threshold is an absolute ±1 kW, with no percentage floor. It obliges an **additional** immediate sample and does not reset the interval: a sample is still due at the periodic bound even when the value has not moved.

`measuredPower` is signed on the generator convention — export positive, import negative — and is mandatory for every resource type. Available power is headroom from the current operating point, so a setpoint is only interpretable against a measured position.

`siteImport` and `siteExport` describe the site's metered connection point rather than the resource. They exist for monitoring and settlement and are **not** a dispatch input: available power is the authoritative dispatch limit. The platform does not track which resources share a site, so co-sited resources carry no cross-resource consistency obligation.

EV chargers are exempt from site import and export, because an OCPP backend typically has no site-meter access.

<Warning>
  `stateOfCharge`, `availableEnergyUp`, and `availableEnergyDown` are not derivable from one another. Reservations outside the platform's control move the usable floor and ceiling independently of the state of charge. Publish all three as sampled values.
</Warning>

Resources on the deprecated v1 EV charger and heat pump APIs keep the metrics those specs define. See [Migrating to v2](/distributed-resources/migration) for the field mapping, including the sign flip on EV active power and the recomputation of heat pump downward capability.

## Message volume

For a battery, with every metric at its bound:

| Prequalified for | Messages per second per asset |
|------------------|-------------------------------|
| `ffr` | ~43 |
| `afrr` | 7 |
| `mfrr` | ~3.1 |
| `wholesale` | ~3.1 |

Available energy and state of charge are market-independent, so about 3 messages per second is the floor for a battery regardless of what it sells. The saving from a slow market comes from active power, available power, and the baseline, which follow the market data frequency.

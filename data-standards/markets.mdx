## Requirements per market

Every bound below is a maximum. Publishing faster or responding sooner is always acceptable.

| Market | Synchronous area | Data frequency | Roundtrip asset response | Delivery lag |
|--------|------------------|----------------|--------------------------|--------------|
| `ffr` | Nordic | 100 ms | 1.2 s | 250 ms |
| `fcr-d` | Nordic | 1 s | 2 s | 250 ms |
| `fcr-n` | Nordic | 1 s | 2 s | 250 ms |
| `fcr` | Continental Europe | 1 s | 2 s | 250 ms |
| `afrr` | Nordic, Continental Europe | 1 s | 2 s | 250 ms |
| `mfrr` | Nordic, Continental Europe | 1 min | 2 min | 5 s |
| `wholesale` | Nordic, Continental Europe | 1 min | 2 min | 5 s |

An asset or resource takes the bounds of the strictest market it is prequalified for, and holds them continuously. See [Requirement resolution](/data-standards/overview#requirement-resolution).

`wholesale` covers day-ahead, intraday continuous, and intentional imbalance. All three settle over 15-minute periods, so they share one set of bounds.

### Data frequency

Data frequency is the maximum gap between consecutive samples of the same metric for the same asset or resource. It applies to active power and to every metric that tracks it. Slow-moving state metrics carry their own fixed bounds, listed in [Metrics](/data-standards/metrics).

Publish a sample on every interval, including when the value has not changed. The platform treats a gap in the stream as a fault, not as an unchanged value.

Every metric is also **change-driven**: publish immediately when a value crosses its change threshold, without waiting for the next interval. On resources the threshold is ±1 kW on the power metrics — see [Metrics](/data-standards/metrics#distributed-energy-resources). For assets no per-metric threshold is published, so data frequency is the binding requirement until one is.

### Roundtrip asset response

Roundtrip asset response is the time from the platform publishing an activation to the platform receiving a measurement that shows the asset or resource moving toward the commanded value.

Both ends are stamped on the platform's clock. The measurement's own timestamps do not enter the calculation, and a resource's acknowledgement does not count as a response: only a measurement showing the change does.

The bound covers command delivery and the **start** of the response. It does not require the ramp to be complete. An asset or resource that begins responding within the bound and reaches the target over the following seconds meets this requirement. Ramp completion belongs to the TSO's activation profile, below.

Assets send no acknowledgement message. The platform detects the response in the measurement stream you already publish. Resources acknowledge every activation they do not discard as stale, and the acknowledgement carries a reason for non-response that a measurement stream cannot express.

The bound applies only under platform control. Under local control the platform sends no setpoint, so there is nothing to measure from — see [Control topology](/data-standards/overview#control-topology).

Sampling and delivery consume part of every roundtrip bound. Each row satisfies:

$$\text{data frequency} + \text{delivery lag} < \text{roundtrip}$$

What remains is the asset's or resource's own budget for deciding and actuating.

### Delivery lag

Delivery lag is the time from the sampling timestamp to the platform's own receipt timestamp. The sampling timestamp is `assetTimestamp` on an asset and `resourceTimestamp` on a resource, with `serverTimestamp` in its place when it is `null` — see [Timestamps](/data-standards/compliance#timestamps). The bound is the same whether the site connects to the broker directly or through your own cloud. It includes clock offset, so your time source must meet the accuracy in [Clocks](/data-standards/compliance#clocks).

Slower markets carry a looser bound because their telemetry passes through more hops. On an EV charger, delivery lag includes the charge point's own OCPP reporting delay, which neither you nor the platform controls.

## Price zones

Market availability follows the synchronous area, not the individual price zone. A zone inherits every market its area offers.

| Price zone | Synchronous area | Markets available |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|
| `DK1` | Continental Europe | `fcr`, `afrr`, `mfrr`, `wholesale` |
| `DK2` | Nordic | `ffr`, `fcr-d`, `fcr-n`, `afrr`, `mfrr`, `wholesale` |

Hybrid Greentech operates in `DK1` and `DK2`. Nordic products are harmonised across the area, so their bounds hold unchanged in any Nordic zone the platform adds later.

<Warning>
  `fcr` and `fcr-n` are not variants of one product. `fcr` is a Continental Europe product available in `DK1`. `fcr-n` is a Nordic product available in `DK2`. An asset or resource cannot substitute one for the other.
</Warning>

## TSO activation profiles

Full activation obligations belong to the TSO. The roundtrip bound is Hybrid Greentech's own requirement, and it is deliberately tighter than the obligation it protects.

| Market | Full activation obligation |
|--------|----------------------------|
| `ffr` | 0.7–1.3 s, depending on the variant and frequency threshold |
| `fcr-d` | 86% of committed response within 7.5 s, and 3.2 s of energy within 7.5 s |
| `fcr-n` | 63% within 60 s, 95% within 3 min |
| `fcr` | Reaction within 30 s |
| `afrr` | 5 min |
| `mfrr` | 12.5 min |
| `wholesale` | As fast as the asset or resource allows |

<Note>
  These are the TSO's figures, not Hybrid Greentech's, and the TSO may revise them. For Denmark, confirm against Energinet's current product overview and prequalification documents before committing capacity.
</Note>

Meeting a roundtrip bound does not discharge the activation obligation. The roundtrip proves the asset or resource started responding. The profile decides whether it delivered.
